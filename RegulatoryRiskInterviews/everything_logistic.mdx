### Logistic PD Modelling Cheatsheet

#### 1. Logistic Function
- **Formula:**  

  $$ P(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}} $$

- **Usage:** Transforms any real-valued number into a probability between 0 and 1.
- **Interpretation:** $x$ often represents a linear combination of predictors, i.e., $x = \beta_0 + \beta_1x_1 + \ldots + \beta_nx_n$.

#### 2. ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristic)
- **Definition:**  
  A plot of True Positive Rate (TPR) vs. False Positive Rate (FPR) across various threshold settings.
- **TPR (Sensitivity):** $\frac{TP}{TP + FN}$
- **FPR:** $\frac{FP}{FP + TN}$
- **Usage:** Visual tool to assess the discrimination ability of the model across thresholds.

#### 3. AUC (Area Under the ROC Curve)
- **Definition:**  
  Measures the overall ability of the model to rank predictions correctly.
- **Interpretation:**  
  AUC = 1 indicates perfect discrimination; 0.5 indicates no discrimination (random guess).
- **Relation to Gini:**  
  $\text{Gini} = 2 \times \text{AUC} - 1$

#### 4. Confusion Matrix
- **Structure:**
  |                | Predicted Positive | Predicted Negative |
  |----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
  | Actual Positive| True Positive (TP) | False Negative (FN)|
  | Actual Negative| False Positive (FP)| True Negative (TN) |
- **Metrics Derived:**
  - **Accuracy:** $\frac{TP+TN}{TP+TN+FP+FN}$
  - **Precision:** $\frac{TP}{TP+FP}$
  - **Recall (Sensitivity):** $\frac{TP}{TP+FN}$
  - **Specificity:** $\frac{TN}{TN+FP}$

#### 5. Gini Coefficient
- **Formula:**  
  $\text{Gini} = 2 \times \text{AUC} - 1$
- **Usage:** Commonly used in credit scoring to measure discriminatory power.
- **Interpretation:**  
  Higher values indicate better discriminatory power.

#### 6. Pitfalls and Metric Tricks
- **Threshold Sensitivity:**  
  - Choice of threshold in logistic regression can shift confusion matrix metrics drastically.
- **Imbalanced Data:**  
  - Accuracy may be misleading; high accuracy can hide poor performance on the minority class.
  - ROC/AUC can overstate model performance if the class distribution is skewed.
- **Calibration vs. Discrimination:**  
  - A model with high AUC may still be poorly calibrated, meaning predicted probabilities do not reflect true risks.
- **Overfitting:**  
  - High in-sample performance (AUC, Gini) might not generalize to out-of-sample data.
- **Metric Interdependencies:**  
  - Gini and AUC are mathematically linked; improvements in one directly affect the other.
- **Business Impact Misalignment:**  
  - Metrics can be “tricked” by models that optimize statistical performance but ignore cost-sensitive implications (e.g., false negatives in credit defaults).

#### 7. Additional Considerations
- **Log-Loss:**  
  - Measures the uncertainty of your predictions. Lower log-loss indicates better calibrated probabilities.
- **Calibration Plots:**  
  - Visual tools to assess how predicted probabilities align with observed outcomes.
- **Cross-Validation:**  
  - Essential for verifying model stability and avoiding overfitting.
- **Regularization:**  
  - Techniques (e.g., L1, L2) to prevent overfitting, especially in high-dimensional data.
