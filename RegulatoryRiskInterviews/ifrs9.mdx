


### How does IFRS 9 classify and measure financial instruments?

IFRS 9 classifies financial instruments based on **business model** and **contractual cash flow characteristics** into three categories:  

1. **Amortized Cost (AC)** – Held to collect cash flows; cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest (SPPI).  
   *Example: A bank loan held until maturity.*  
2. **Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income (FVOCI)** – Held both to collect cash flows and for sale; SPPI test passed.  
   *Example: Corporate bonds held for periodic interest and potential sale.*  
3. **Fair Value through Profit or Loss (FVTPL)** – Doesn’t meet AC or FVOCI criteria; held for trading or complex cash flows.  
   *Example: Equity investments or derivatives like options.*  

Financial liabilities are mostly **measured at amortized cost**, except those designated at FVTPL.

### Classify the stages of IFRS9 loans?

IFRS 9's **Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model** has three stages of credit impairment, affecting how ECL is recognized and reported:  

1. **Stage 1 (Performing)** – No significant increase in credit risk; **12-month ECL** is recognized.  
   - *Example: A newly issued loan with no signs of distress.*  
   - **ECL Reporting:** Provision is based on losses expected over the next 12 months.  

2. **Stage 2 (Underperforming)** – Significant increase in credit risk but no default; **Lifetime ECL** is recognized.  
   - *Example: A borrower experiencing financial difficulties but still making payments.*  
   - **ECL Reporting:** Provision covers all expected losses over the loan's remaining life.  

3. **Stage 3 (Non-performing/Impaired)** – Credit is impaired (default or high risk); **Lifetime ECL** recognized, and interest income is based on net carrying amount.  
   - *Example: A loan in default with missed payments beyond 90 days.*  
   - **ECL Reporting:** Provision is fully recognized, and interest is recorded on the loan's net balance (gross amount minus loss allowance).  

### **Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR) under IFRS 9**  
SICR occurs when the credit risk of a financial asset has significantly increased since initial recognition, requiring it to move from **Stage 1 (12-month ECL)** to **Stage 2 (Lifetime ECL)** in the ECL model.  

### How is SICR Assessed?
SICR is not defined by a single rule but is assessed based on multiple quantitative and qualitative factors:  

1. **Quantitative Indicators:**  
   - Increase in **Probability of Default (PD)** beyond a set threshold.  
   - Deterioration in **credit ratings** or risk scores.  
   - **Significant widening of credit spreads.**  

2. **Qualitative Indicators:**  
   - **Breach of covenants** or restructuring discussions.  
   - **Adverse economic conditions** affecting the borrower’s sector.  
   - **Negative internal credit assessments** by risk managers.  

3. **Backstop Criteria (Rebuttable Presumption):**  
   - **More than 30 days past due** automatically signals SICR unless justified otherwise.  


### **Key Differences: Global Simplified IRB vs. Advanced IRB**  

| Feature            | **Global Simplified IRB**        | **Advanced IRB (A-IRB)**         |
|--------------------|---------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Risk Parameter Estimation** | Uses **regulator-prescribed** PD, LGD, EAD | Banks estimate **PD, LGD, EAD** using internal models |
| **Flexibility**  | Standardized, less flexible | Highly flexible, based on internal risk models |
| **Regulatory Approval**  | No approval required | Requires regulatory approval |
| **Complexity**  | Simplified, easier to implement | Complex, requiring strong risk modeling |
| **Capital Requirements**  | Generally higher (more conservative) | Lower if internal models show lower risk |
| **Data Requirements**  | Minimal historical data needed | Extensive historical data required for model validation |
