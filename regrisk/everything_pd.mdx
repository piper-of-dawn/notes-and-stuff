### **Descriptions of Each Metric**

#### **PIT PD (Point-in-Time PD)**
- **Definition:** The probability of default over the next year, reflecting current economic conditions at the start of each period.
- **Use:** Used for real-time risk assessment, loan pricing, and provisioning, sensitive to short-term economic changes.

#### **Survival Probability**
- **Definition:** The probability that the borrower does not default up to a given year, calculated as the product of annual survival rates (1 - PIT PD).
- **Use:** Forms the foundation for cumulative and marginal PDs, essential for modeling long-term credit risk.

#### **Cumulative PD**
- **Definition:** The total probability of default from the start of the loan up to a specific year, derived as 1 minus the survival probability.
- **Use:** Measures overall default risk over time, critical for portfolio-level analysis and regulatory reporting.

#### **Marginal PD**
- **Definition:** The incremental probability of default in a specific year, conditional on survival up to that year, calculated as the difference in consecutive cumulative PDs.
- **Use:** Enables year-by-year expected loss calculations, supporting granular risk management and capital allocation.

---

Through-the-Cycle (TTC) PD is calculated by estimating a long-term average default probability that smooths out economic cycle fluctuations, reflecting a borrower’s intrinsic risk over time. Here’s a concise explanation of the process:

1. **Collect Historical Data:** Gather default rates for a borrower or similar cohort (e.g., industry, rating) over a long period (10-20 years), spanning multiple economic cycles.
2. **Average Default Rates:** Compute the mean annual default rate across this period, neutralizing peaks (booms) and troughs (recessions).
3. **Adjust for Stability:** Optionally, use statistical methods (e.g., regression, smoothing) to strip out cyclical effects, focusing on structural risk factors (e.g., leverage, industry).
4. **Calibration:** Map the result to a rating or PD scale, ensuring consistency with observed long-term defaults (e.g., Basel IRB calibration).

**Example:** If annual default rates for a firm’s rating bucket were 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 0.8%, and 1.5% over 5 years, TTC PD might be approximated as the average (1.16%), adjusted to reflect cycle-independent risk.

**Key Point:** TTC PD is less volatile than PIT PD, making it ideal for regulatory capital purposes (e.g., Basel), as it avoids procyclicality.
