title: "Default Modelling"
---

Modeling the Probability of Default (PD) for a corporate borrower is a critical component of wholesale credit risk management. Below, I’ll provide a structured explanation of the process, tailored to a senior-level understanding, with clear steps and considerations you can use to demonstrate expertise in your interview.

---

### **1. Define Probability of Default (PD)**
- **What it is:** PD represents the likelihood that a corporate borrower will default on its debt obligations over a specific time horizon (e.g., 1 year).
- **Context:** It’s a key input in credit risk models, such as Basel II/III frameworks, internal ratings-based (IRB) approaches, and expected loss calculations (EL = PD × LGD × EAD).
- **Objective:** Accurately estimate PD to assess risk, set capital reserves, and price loans.

---

### **2. Choose a Modeling Approach**
There are several established methodologies to model PD. Select based on data availability, regulatory requirements, and the firm’s risk appetite. Common approaches include:

#### **A. Statistical Models (Scorecard-Based)**
- **Description:** Use historical data to build a logistic regression or similar model.
- **Inputs:** Financial ratios, qualitative factors, and macroeconomic variables.
- **Process:**
  1. Collect data on past defaults and non-defaults.
  2. Identify predictive variables (e.g., leverage, interest coverage, cash flow).
  3. Fit a model (e.g., logit: PD = 1 / (1 + e^(-z)), where z = β₀ + β₁X₁ + ... + βₙXₙ).
  4. Validate using metrics like AUC (Area Under the Curve) or Gini coefficient.

#### **B. Structural Models (Merton Model)**
- **Description:** Based on option pricing theory, where default occurs if the firm’s asset value falls below its debt obligations.
- **Inputs:** Market data (equity value, volatility), balance sheet data (debt levels).
- **Process:**
  1. Estimate asset value and volatility using equity as a call option on assets.
  2. Calculate the distance to default (DD = (Asset Value - Debt) / Asset Volatility).
  3. Map DD to PD using a normal distribution assumption.

#### **C. Expert Judgment (Rating-Based)**
- **Description:** Assign internal credit ratings based on qualitative and quantitative factors, then map ratings to PD using historical default rates.
- **Inputs:** Analyst assessments, industry trends, financial metrics.
- **Process:** Calibrate PDs to observed default frequencies per rating bucket.

#### **D. Machine Learning Models**
- **Description:** Use advanced techniques (e.g., random forests, neural networks) for higher predictive power.
- **Inputs:** Large datasets including non-linear relationships and unstructured data.
- **Process:** Train and test the model, ensuring interpretability for regulatory compliance.

---

### **3. Gather and Prepare Data**
- **Financial Data:**
  - Balance sheet (e.g., debt-to-equity, liquidity ratios).
  - Income statement (e.g., EBITDA, interest coverage).
  - Cash flow statement (e.g., free cash flow).
- **Market Data:**
  - Stock price volatility, CDS spreads (if available).
- **Macroeconomic Variables:**
  - GDP growth, interest rates, industry-specific indices.
- **Qualitative Factors:**
  - Management quality, competitive position, regulatory environment.
- **Data Quality:** Ensure completeness, consistency, and relevance. Address missing data through imputation or exclusion.

---

### **4. Model Development**
- **Step 1: Variable Selection**
  - Use statistical tests (e.g., p-values, correlation analysis) or domain expertise to select predictors.
  - Avoid multicollinearity (e.g., check VIF - Variance Inflation Factor).
- **Step 2: Model Calibration**
  - Fit the model to historical data (e.g., 5-10 years of default history).
  - Adjust for economic cycles (point-in-time vs. through-the-cycle PD).
- **Step 3: Output**
  - PD as a percentage (e.g., 2% annual default probability).
  - Ensure granularity (e.g., by industry, size, or rating).

---

### **5. Validation and Backtesting**
- **Validation:**
  - Split data into training and testing sets.
  - Assess discriminatory power (e.g., ROC curve) and calibration (e.g., Hosmer-Lemeshow test).
- **Backtesting:**
  - Compare predicted PDs to actual default rates over time.
  - Adjust for overfitting or bias.
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Ensure alignment with Basel standards or local regulations (e.g., stress testing).

---

### **6. Adjust for Portfolio and Economic Context**
- **Portfolio Effects:** Account for correlation between borrowers (e.g., industry concentration risk).
- **Stress Testing:** Simulate PD under adverse scenarios (e.g., recession, rising rates).
- **Through-the-Cycle (TTC) vs. Point-in-Time (PIT):**
  - TTC PD: Long-term average, stable across cycles.
  - PIT PD: Reflects current economic conditions.

---

### **7. Practical Implementation**
- **Integration:** Embed PD into credit approval, pricing, and capital allocation processes.
- **Monitoring:** Regularly update PDs based on new financials or market conditions.
- **Documentation:** Maintain transparency for auditors and regulators.

---

### **Key Considerations for Interview**
- **Senior-Level Insight:** Highlight trade-offs (e.g., accuracy vs. interpretability in ML models, PIT vs. TTC calibration).
- **Regulatory Nuances:** Mention Basel IRB requirements (e.g., 0.03% PD floor) or IFRS 9 expected credit loss standards.
- **Business Impact:** Link PD modeling to loan pricing (e.g., risk-adjusted return on capital) and portfolio management.

---

### **Example Walkthrough**
Suppose you’re modeling PD for a manufacturing firm:
1. **Data:** Debt/EBITDA = 4x, interest coverage = 2x, GDP growth = 1%.
2. **Approach:** Logistic regression.
3. **Model:** PD = 1 / (1 + e^(-(β₀ + β₁×Debt/EBITDA + β₂×Interest Coverage + β₃×GDP))).
4. **Calibration:** Historical data shows a 3% default rate for similar firms; tweak coefficients accordingly.
5. **Result:** PD = 2.5% (1-year horizon).
